# Study Plan — Technology / AI Consultant (consulting firms and systems integrators)

For engineers and technical leads moving into technology or AI consulting roles at strategy firms, professional services firms and systems integrators; the default plan assumes 6 weeks at about 10–12 hours a week, with a 2-week crash version below.

## Before you start

Spend 30 minutes rating yourself on each area (1 = no experience, 5 = could teach it). Skim or skip the weeks that cover areas where you are already a 4 or 5, and spend the saved time on your lowest scores.

| Skill area | Rate yourself 1–5 | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Case structuring | | You can produce a tailored, MECE issue tree for an unfamiliar business problem in two minutes and explain where to start |
| Case maths and estimation | | You can do percentages, growth, break-even and market sizing aloud, accurately, with sanity checks |
| Synthesis and slides | | You give answer-first recommendations and write slide titles that state conclusions |
| AI strategy and use-case prioritisation | | You can lay out an AI strategy, a value-feasibility matrix and a readiness assessment from memory |
| AI technical literacy | | You can explain RAG, agents, fine-tuning, evaluation, cost and risk to a non-technical executive and to an engineer |
| Solution architecture | | You can whiteboard an AI solution with integrations, security and a phased delivery plan |
| Workshops and stakeholder management | | You have run workshops with senior people and handled open disagreement |
| Proposals and commercials | | You can outline a proposal, choose a pricing model and estimate effort with assumptions |
| Delivery leadership (including offshore) | | You have led a team across locations to a deadline, handled scope and escalation |
| Fit stories (personal impact, leadership) | | You can withstand 10–15 minutes of detailed follow-up on each of four stories |

## 6-week plan

### Week 1 — Consulting basics and case structuring

**Goals:** understand how consulting works and learn the core case method.

- [ ] Read the role overview, firm types and loop in [README.md](./README.md#the-role) and [Typical interview loop](./README.md#typical-interview-loop).
- [ ] Study [Role and firm fit](./README.md#role-and-firm-fit-1) model answers.
- [ ] Study the method answers in [Case interviews and structured problem solving](./README.md#case-interviews-and-structured-problem-solving-1) (approach, MECE, hypotheses, pyramid principle).
- [ ] Read [04 Decomposition](../../topics/04-decomposition/ANSWERS.md), especially [commercial / enterprise](../../topics/04-decomposition/ANSWERS.md#c-commercial--enterprise) and [interviewer push-backs](../../topics/04-decomposition/ANSWERS.md#f-typical-interviewer-follow-ups--push-backs).
- [ ] Read [COMMON-QUESTIONS.md](../COMMON-QUESTIONS.md), Tier 1.

**Deliverables:**
1. Issue trees for ten different business questions (profitability, growth, cost reduction, market entry, AI adoption), written in 5 minutes each.
2. A one-paragraph "why consulting, why this firm" answer for each target firm.

**Mock:** two cases with a partner (one candidate-led, one interviewer-led), using the three case prompts in the role file.

**End of week check:** you can open any case with a clear objective, two clarifying questions and a tailored structure within three minutes.

### Week 2 — Case volume and estimation

**Goals:** build speed and accuracy through repetition.

- [ ] Study [Estimation and quantitative](./README.md#estimation-and-quantitative-1) and redo each estimate without looking.
- [ ] Practise reading charts and stating the "so what" in one sentence.
- [ ] Daily 15-minute mental maths drill (percentages, multiplication with zeros, growth rates).
- [ ] Try the case examples many firms publish on their careers pages (for example McKinsey and BCG both publish practice material).

**Deliverables:**
1. Six market-sizing or savings estimates written up with assumptions, a range and key drivers.
2. A personal case log: case, score out of 5 per skill (structure, maths, insight, synthesis), and the one thing to fix.

**Mock:** four live cases with at least two different partners; record one and review it.

**End of week check:** you complete a 30-minute case with no maths errors and end with a one-minute pyramid synthesis.

### Week 3 — AI strategy, roadmaps and responsible AI

**Goals:** speak with authority about AI strategy at executive level.

- [ ] Study [AI strategy and roadmap](./README.md#ai-strategy-and-roadmap-1).
- [ ] Read [06 AI / LLM part 2, safety, security and responsible AI](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS-part2.md#h-safety-security-and-responsible-ai) and [fine-tuning and customisation](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS-part2.md#f-fine-tuning-and-model-customisation).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios, business acumen](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#e-business-acumen).
- [ ] Read public summaries of the EU AI Act risk categories and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, plus the data protection law relevant to your market.

**Deliverables:**
1. A one-page AI strategy framework, a value-feasibility matrix template and a readiness heat map you can draw from memory.
2. A five-slide executive summary for a fictional client's AI roadmap, with conclusion-style slide titles.

**Mock:** an AI strategy case: "a bank asks for an AI strategy; where would you start?", followed by challenge questions on risk and ROI.

**End of week check:** you can explain how you would move a client from pilots to production in three minutes, with concrete governance steps.

### Week 4 — Technical depth and solution design

**Goals:** pass the technical round on technology tracks and make your strategy answers credible.

- [ ] Review [06 AI / LLM](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md): [RAG](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#c-rag-retrieval-augmented-generation), [agents](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#d-agents-and-tool-use), [evaluation](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#e-evaluation-most-important).
- [ ] Practise two prompts from [03 System design, applied prompts](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md#a-applied--customer-context-design-prompts) and [AI / LLM prompts](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md#b-ai--llm-system-design-prompts).
- [ ] Review [08 Cloud and integration](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md) and [07 Data and SQL](../../topics/07-data-and-sql/ANSWERS.md) at a skim level.
- [ ] Study the claims-processing whiteboard in [Delivery](./README.md#delivery-1).
- [ ] For Thoughtworks-style or engineering-heavy loops: two timed sessions from [02 Coding](../../topics/02-coding/ANSWERS.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. A small working demo: a document extraction or knowledge-assistant prototype with a 30-case evaluation set and a cost estimate.
2. A one-page solution design with a phased delivery plan and effort estimate.

**Mock:** a 45-minute technical interview: explain RAG and evaluation to a "client", then whiteboard the solution and estimate the team.

**End of week check:** you can whiteboard an AI solution and turn it into phases, team and effort in 30 minutes.

### Week 5 — Workshops, proposals, delivery and fit stories

**Goals:** cover the manager-level rounds and the fit interview.

- [ ] Study [Client workshops and stakeholder management](./README.md#client-workshops-and-stakeholder-management-1), [Proposals and commercial](./README.md#proposals-and-commercial-1) and [Delivery](./README.md#delivery-1).
- [ ] Study [Behavioral (consulting fit)](./README.md#behavioral-consulting-fit-1) and [10 Behavioral](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md), especially [conflict and collaboration](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#conflict--collaboration) and [communication](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#communication).
- [ ] Fill gaps in [my story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) using the [story bank checklist](../../topics/00-overview/ANSWERS.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. A two-page proposal for an AI engagement (approach, team, timeline, pricing model, assumptions, risks).
2. A half-day workshop agenda with exercises and expected outputs.

**Mock:** a fit interview where the partner spends 15 minutes on one story asking "what exactly did you do and say?", plus a short proposal presentation.

**End of week check:** each of your four main stories survives deep follow-up with specific actions, words and results.

### Week 6 — Firm-specific preparation and full mocks

**Goals:** tailor everything to each firm and rehearse the whole loop.

- [ ] Research each firm's AI practice, published points of view, level structure and interview process on its careers site; use [11 Company-specific](../../topics/11-company-specific/ANSWERS.md) as a research checklist.
- [ ] Prepare questions from [14 Questions to ask](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md), adding practice, staffing and utilisation questions.
- [ ] Answer every item in [Role-specific questions](./README.md#role-specific-questions) aloud in under three minutes.
- [ ] Work through the [Prep checklist](./README.md#prep-checklist).

**Deliverables:**
1. A one-page sheet per firm: why this firm, relevant offerings, people you spoke to, three questions.
2. A final case log showing at least 15–20 cases completed, with your weakest skill improved.

**Mock:** a full loop: two cases, one fit interview, one technical round, and a manager-round case presentation, spread over two days.

**End of week check:** mock interviewers would pass you on case, fit and technical rounds at your target level.

## Daily routine

A realistic 90-minute day:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 min | Mental maths and one quick estimation drill |
| 15 min | Build one issue tree for a new business question, then compare it with a model structure |
| 40 min | Main study block for the week's theme, or a live case with a partner |
| 15 min | Say one fit story or model answer aloud and time it |
| 5 min | Update the case log and plan tomorrow |

Live cases are the most valuable activity; on days with a case partner, let the case replace the main block.

## Mock interview schedule

| Week | Mocks | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 cases | A case partner (often found through consulting prep communities or friends who have consulted) |
| 2 | 4 cases, one recorded | At least two different partners |
| 3 | 1 AI strategy case, 1 standard case | A partner; ideally someone who works in consulting |
| 4 | 1 technical round | A senior engineer or architect |
| 5 | 1 fit interview with deep follow-ups, 1 proposal presentation, 2 cases | A current or former consultant if possible |
| 6 | Full loop over two days | Different people per round |

Options: case partners from your network or online case-practice communities; current or former consultants (alumni networks are a good source); paid coaching or platforms such as Exponent for structured case and product practice, and Pramp or interviewing.io for technical rounds. Aim for at least 15–20 live cases before strategy-firm interviews, fewer for systems integrators whose loops are more technical and behavioral.

## Fast track for the repo owner's profile

- **Skim:** delivery and estimation fundamentals; you already run sprint planning, release plans, monthly programme reports and onboarding plans, which map to the delivery and managerial rounds.
- **Skim:** technical solution design for integrations and data; contract-first APIs, an API gateway with JWT authentication and rate limiting, CRM and accounting-system integrations and a large bidirectional MySQL-to-Postgres migration with reconciliation give strong material for the solution architecture round.
- **Biggest gap 1, case interviews:** no evidence of case practice. Weeks 1–2 are the priority if you target strategy firms or their AI arms; for systems integrators and technology consultancies, a lighter version is usually enough.
- **Biggest gap 2, production AI depth:** no production LLM, RAG, agent or evaluation experience yet. Week 4's demo gives you something real to talk about and makes your strategy answers credible.
- **Biggest gap 3, external client work and selling:** most stakeholders have been internal (sales, finance, academic teams). Present them honestly as internal clients, and prepare the proposal and RFP answers carefully because there is no direct evidence of selling.
- **AI strategy and build-versus-buy rounds:** use the speech-to-text provider evaluation (a contribution, not sole authorship) with its buy-versus-build cost model and India DPDP Act constraints.
- **Business case and estimation rounds:** use the commerce platform work (SKU catalogue, discount approval rules engine, invoicing and payments tied to a cash-collection metric) to show you link technology to financial outcomes.
- **Fit interview (leadership, personal impact):** use leading a squad of about 4 engineers, mentoring, the legacy modernisation (PHP 5.3 to 7.3 user migration) and influencing finance or sales stakeholders on process changes.
- **Target firms:** being based in India, Indian IT services firms and the India offices and delivery centres of global firms are natural targets; confirm level mapping, since a Principal Engineer title may map to manager or senior manager depending on the firm.
- Keep all stories in [my story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) and use only facts you can confirm.

## Resources

- **Case interviews:** "Case in Point" by Marc Cosentino; "Case Interview Secrets" by Victor Cheng; the practice cases and interview guidance on McKinsey, BCG and Bain careers sites.
- **Structured thinking and communication:** "The Pyramid Principle" by Barbara Minto; "Bulletproof Problem Solving" by Charles Conn and Robert McLean; "The McKinsey Way" by Ethan Rasiel.
- **AI strategy:** "Competing in the Age of AI" by Marco Iansiti and Karim Lakhani; "Prediction Machines" by Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans and Avi Goldfarb.
- **AI engineering:** "AI Engineering" by Chip Huyen; official model provider documentation such as the [Anthropic documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com) and the [OpenAI platform documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs).
- **Governance and regulation:** the NIST AI Risk Management Framework; the European Commission's EU AI Act materials; ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems); India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
- **Consulting delivery:** "The Trusted Advisor" by David Maister, Charles Green and Robert Galford; "Flawless Consulting" by Peter Block.
- **Cloud:** the [AWS Well-Architected Framework](https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/well-architected/) and the equivalent Azure and Google Cloud frameworks.
