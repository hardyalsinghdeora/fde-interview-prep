# Study Plan — Staff / Principal FDE, Principal SA, Field CTO and Head of Forward Deployed Engineering

For experienced engineers and technical leaders preparing for senior customer-facing leadership loops; the default plan assumes 6 weeks at about 10–12 hours a week, with a 2-week crash version below.

## Before you start

Spend 30 minutes rating yourself on each area (1 = no experience, 5 = could teach it). Skim or skip the weeks that cover areas where you are already a 4 or 5, and put the saved time into your lowest scores.

| Skill area | Rate yourself 1–5 | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership narrative and scope | | You can state in two minutes the scope you have owned (teams, systems, customers, budget), your results, and why this role is the next step, with accurate numbers |
| Strategic account management | | You have owned a multi-quarter plan for a large customer or programme and can show a stakeholder map, risks and an expansion path |
| Executive communication | | You can present an answer-first, 20-minute recommendation to executives and handle hostile questions without losing the thread |
| Business case and ROI | | You can build a conservative ROI model with a baseline, costs, payback and a sensitivity range in under 30 minutes |
| Deployment methodology | | You can describe stages, gates, artefacts and metrics for repeatable deployments, and how you would decide product versus custom work |
| Team building and hiring | | You have hired and developed engineers, designed parts of an interview loop, and handled a performance problem |
| Product influence | | You can show a time field or user evidence changed a roadmap, and describe the process you used |
| Enterprise AI architecture | | You can whiteboard an enterprise LLM platform (gateway, retrieval with access control, tools, evaluation, governance) and defend trade-offs |
| AI / LLM depth | | You can explain RAG, agents, evaluation, fine-tuning, cost and safety precisely, and have built at least one LLM application |
| Hands-on coding (for AI-lab loops) | | You can solve a practical coding task in 45 minutes in your main language while talking through it |

## 6-week plan

### Week 1 — Role map, leadership narrative and story bank

**Goals:** understand what this level is judged on and build the raw material for every behavioral round.

- [ ] Read the role overview and loop in [README.md](./README.md#the-role) and [Typical interview loop](./README.md#typical-interview-loop).
- [ ] Compare with the neighbouring roles: [01 Forward Deployed Engineer](../01-forward-deployed-engineer/README.md), [02 Deployment Strategist](../02-deployment-strategist/README.md), [04 Solutions Architect](../04-solutions-architect/README.md).
- [ ] Read [COMMON-QUESTIONS.md](../COMMON-QUESTIONS.md), Tier 1 first.
- [ ] Work through [10 Behavioral answers](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md), especially ownership, conflict and hiring-manager topics.
- [ ] Use the [story bank checklist](../../topics/00-overview/ANSWERS.md) to fill gaps in [my story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md).
- [ ] Draft answers to the first two themes: [Role and scope](./README.md#role-and-scope-1) model answers.

**Deliverables:**
1. A one-page leadership narrative (scope, results, why this role, why now).
2. Eight leadership stories in STAR form, each with scope and a measurable result.

**Mock:** a 30-minute hiring-manager mock with a peer: "Walk me through your career" plus two leadership stories, with follow-ups.

**End of week check:** you can tell your leadership narrative in two minutes and any of eight stories in three minutes without notes.

### Week 2 — Strategic accounts and executive communication

**Goals:** think and speak like the senior technical owner of an account.

- [ ] Study [Strategic accounts and account strategy](./README.md#strategic-accounts-and-account-strategy-1) and [Executive advisory and presentations](./README.md#executive-advisory-and-presentations-1).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md), especially [difficult situations](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#b-difficult-situations), [communication](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#c-communication) and [business acumen](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#e-business-acumen).
- [ ] Practise two enterprise cases from [04 Decomposition, commercial / enterprise](../../topics/04-decomposition/ANSWERS.md#c-commercial--enterprise) as account-strategy cases.
- [ ] Build an ROI model for one AI use case using the structure in the CFO model answer.

**Deliverables:**
1. A sample multi-quarter account plan (two pages) for a realistic customer.
2. A 20-minute executive presentation (about ten slides, answer-first), recorded once.

**Mock:** an executive presentation mock where the peer plays CFO, CISO and COO and interrupts.

**End of week check:** you can present the deck in 12 minutes, leave time for questions, and answer "what is the ROI?" with a baseline, costs and payback.

### Week 3 — Enterprise AI architecture and technical credibility

**Goals:** be the most technically credible person in a senior loop, including any coding round.

- [ ] Review [06 AI / LLM](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md): [RAG](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#c-rag-retrieval-augmented-generation), [agents](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#d-agents-and-tool-use) and [evaluation](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#e-evaluation-most-important).
- [ ] Review [part 2](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS-part2.md): [production LLM engineering](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS-part2.md#g-production-llm-engineering) and [safety and responsible AI](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS-part2.md#h-safety-security-and-responsible-ai).
- [ ] Practise two prompts from [03 System design, AI / LLM prompts](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md#b-ai--llm-system-design-prompts).
- [ ] Study [Technical depth at principal level](./README.md#technical-depth-at-principal-level-1).
- [ ] If the loop includes coding: two timed sessions from [02 Coding](../../topics/02-coding/ANSWERS.md).

**Deliverables:**
1. Build a small RAG or agent application with an evaluation set of 30–50 cases and a cost estimate; write a one-page design note.
2. Draw the enterprise LLM platform reference architecture from memory and write the trade-offs.

**Mock:** a 45-minute architecture deep dive with a senior engineer: "design an LLM platform for a regulated enterprise", with operating-model follow-ups.

**End of week check:** you can whiteboard the platform in 25 minutes and explain how evaluation gates, access control and cost attribution work.

### Week 4 — Deployment methodology, metrics and product influence

**Goals:** have a clear, written point of view on how deployments should run and how the field shapes the product.

- [ ] Study [Deployment methodology](./README.md#deployment-methodology-1) and [Product and roadmap influence](./README.md#product-and-roadmap-influence-1).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios, product feedback and scale](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#d-product-feedback--scale).
- [ ] Read [12 Debugging](../../topics/12-debugging-troubleshooting/ANSWERS.md) to prepare for escalation questions.
- [ ] Research how the target company describes its field or FDE organisation in [11 Company-specific](../../topics/11-company-specific/ANSWERS.md) and in public talks.

**Deliverables:**
1. A one-page deployment methodology (stages, gates, artefacts, metrics, product-versus-custom rule).
2. A field-to-product feedback process on one page, with the metrics you would track.

**Mock:** a cross-functional panel mock: one peer plays a product leader who refuses a feature, another a sales leader who wants to over-promise.

**End of week check:** you can explain your methodology and your metrics in five minutes and defend them against a sceptical product leader.

### Week 5 — Building and leading the team; commercial leadership

**Goals:** show you can build, run and grow a field organisation.

- [ ] Study [Building and leading the team](./README.md#building-and-leading-the-team-1) and [Commercial and cross-functional leadership](./README.md#commercial-and-cross-functional-leadership-1).
- [ ] Re-read [10 Behavioral, hiring-manager round topics](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#hiring-manager-round-topics) and [conflict and collaboration](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#conflict--collaboration).
- [ ] Prepare the [Leadership behavioral](./README.md#leadership-behavioral-1) stories with your own facts.

**Deliverables:**
1. A 90-day plan for the specific role you are interviewing for.
2. A hiring plan and interview loop design for a forward deployed team (roles, rounds, rubrics).

**Mock:** a 45-minute leadership behavioral mock with deep follow-ups ("What would you do differently?", "What did your manager say?").

**End of week check:** you can answer every leadership question with a story that shows scope beyond your own team and a measurable result.

### Week 6 — Full-loop rehearsal and company-specific polish

**Goals:** combine everything under realistic conditions.

- [ ] Re-run your weakest areas from the self-assessment.
- [ ] Review the whole [Role-specific questions](./README.md#role-specific-questions) list and answer each aloud in under three minutes.
- [ ] Prepare questions for each interviewer from [14 Questions to ask](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Work through the [Prep checklist](./README.md#prep-checklist).

**Deliverables:**
1. A final version of the executive presentation, rehearsed three times.
2. A one-page "interview day sheet": narrative, eight story titles, methodology summary, questions to ask.

**Mock:** a full mock loop over two days: hiring manager, executive presentation, architecture, behavioral (four sessions with different people if possible).

**End of week check:** you can go through a full loop without notes and your mock interviewers would hire you at the target level.

## Daily routine

A realistic 90-minute day:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 min | Say one story or model answer aloud and time it; refine the weakest sentence |
| 45 min | Main study block for the week's theme (reading, writing a plan or deck, or a build task) |
| 20 min | Practice: whiteboard a design, rehearse part of the presentation, or a short coding problem if the loop needs it |
| 10 min | Write down what you learned, update the story bank and plan tomorrow |

On two days a week, replace the main block with a mock interview and its review.

## Mock interview schedule

| Week | Mock | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hiring manager: career walk-through and two stories | A peer or former manager |
| 2 | Executive presentation with interruptions | A peer playing executives; record it |
| 3 | Architecture deep dive | A senior engineer or architect |
| 4 | Cross-functional panel (product and sales conflict) | Two peers, ideally a product manager and someone in sales |
| 5 | Leadership behavioral with deep follow-ups | A manager or senior leader you trust |
| 6 | Full loop over two days | Different people per round if possible |

Options: peers and former colleagues are the best match for senior leadership mocks. Paid platforms such as interviewing.io, Exponent or Pramp can help for coding and system design practice; recording yourself on video is the fastest way to fix presentation habits. Ask every mock interviewer the same question at the end: "At what level would you hire me, and why?"

## Fast track for the repo owner's profile

- **Be realistic about the target level:** with about 15 years of experience as a Principal Software Engineer leading a small squad, Staff or Principal FDE and Principal Solutions Architect roles are the strongest fit; Field CTO and Head of FDE roles usually expect years of external customer leadership and larger teams, so treat them as stretch applications.
- **Skim:** API and integration architecture, data migration and delivery leadership material; these are strengths (contract-first integrations, an API gateway with JWT authentication and rate limiting, a large bidirectional MySQL-to-Postgres migration with reconciliation, sprint and release planning).
- **Biggest gap 1, external enterprise customers:** most stakeholder experience is internal (sales, finance, academic teams). Reframe those stories as "internal customers" honestly, and spend extra time on Weeks 2 and 4 to build the account-strategy and executive vocabulary.
- **Biggest gap 2, production AI:** no production LLM, RAG, agent or evaluation experience yet. Week 3's build deliverable is essential, not optional; it gives you something real to discuss in the architecture round.
- **Biggest gap 3, executive presentations and team scale:** practise the Week 2 presentation more than once, and prepare a clear answer to "How would you lead a team much larger than your current squad?"
- **Strategic accounts and executive advisory rounds:** use the order, subscription and commerce platform work (SKU catalogue, discount approval rules engine, invoicing tied to a cash-collection metric) as evidence of business-outcome thinking.
- **Deployment methodology and product influence rounds:** use the legacy modernisation and migration work (ordering constraints, idempotency guards, reconciliation) as evidence of repeatable, safe delivery methods.
- **Build-versus-buy and AI advisory questions:** use the speech-to-text provider evaluation (a contribution, not sole ownership) with its cost model and data-protection constraints under India's DPDP Act.
- **Team building questions:** use mentoring, onboarding plans and monthly programme reports as evidence; be clear about team size.
- Keep all stories in [my story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) and use only facts you can confirm.

## Resources

- **Books on senior technical leadership:** "The Staff Engineer's Path" by Tanya Reilly; "Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track" by Will Larson; "An Elegant Puzzle" by Will Larson; "The Manager's Path" by Camille Fournier.
- **Strategy and product:** "Good Strategy Bad Strategy" by Richard Rumelt; "Inspired" by Marty Cagan; "Crossing the Chasm" by Geoffrey Moore.
- **Communication:** "The Pyramid Principle" by Barbara Minto (answer-first writing and presenting).
- **Hiring:** "Who: The A Method for Hiring" by Geoff Smart and Randy Street.
- **Enterprise selling vocabulary:** "The Challenger Sale" by Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson; the MEDDICC qualification framework (widely described online).
- **AI architecture and evaluation:** "AI Engineering" by Chip Huyen; the official documentation of the model providers the company uses, such as the [Anthropic documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com) and the [OpenAI platform documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs).
- **Cloud architecture:** the [AWS Well-Architected Framework](https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/well-architected/) and its Generative AI Lens.
- **Risk and governance:** the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the EU AI Act summaries published by the European Commission.
