# Study Plan — Technical Delivery Manager

For engineers, tech leads and project managers preparing for technical delivery manager, engagement manager or technical program manager interviews on customer deployments; assumes about 10–12 hours a week for six weeks, or a two-week crash plan if an interview is already booked.

## Before you start

Spend 30 minutes rating yourself on each area. Skip or skim the weeks where you are already a 4 or 5, and put the time into your weakest two areas.

| Skill area | Rate yourself 1–5 | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and critical path | | You can turn a brief into workstreams, phases, milestones and a critical path in 20 minutes, with three-point estimates and contingency. |
| Risk, issue and dependency management | | You can build a RAID log with specific, owned entries and explain the four risk responses with examples. |
| Status reporting | | You can write a one-page status report with objective RAG criteria and decisions needed. |
| Governance and executive communication | | You can design a steering committee and working group structure and present a red project with options and a recommendation. |
| Scope and change control | | You can walk through a change control process and handle a "this was always in scope" dispute calmly. |
| Budget and commercials | | You can calculate CPI, SPI and estimate at completion, and explain fixed price versus time and materials. |
| Agile at scale | | You can explain scrum, kanban and SAFe (ART, PI planning, ROAM) and coordinate several teams on one release. |
| Escalations and incidents | | You can write a clear escalation and describe your role in a severity-1 incident. |
| Technical understanding | | You can draw an integration architecture and name its cutover and data migration risks. |
| Behavioural stories | | You have six or more STAR stories with numbers: failing project, bad news, conflict, competing priorities, vendor issue, scope dispute. |

## 6-week plan

### Week 1 — The role, planning foundations and your stories

**Goals:** understand what delivery interviews score; learn a repeatable way to plan a multi-team deployment; start your story bank.

- [ ] Read the role overview and loop in [this role file](./README.md) and the comparison with other roles in [the roles index](../README.md).
- [ ] Read "Role and motivation" and "Planning multi-team deployments" in [this file's model answers](./README.md#model-answers).
- [ ] Read [01 Recruiter & motivation answers](../../topics/01-recruiter-and-motivation/ANSWERS.md) and adapt "why this role" into "why delivery management".
- [ ] Read [COMMON-QUESTIONS.md](../COMMON-QUESTIONS.md) and mark which apply.
- [ ] Use [04 Decomposition answers](../../topics/04-decomposition/ANSWERS.md) to practise breaking a vague goal into workstreams.
- [ ] Start your stories in [the story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) using [00 Overview story checklist](../../topics/00-overview/README.md).

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A one-page delivery plan for a fictional deployment (one platform, five business units, three internal teams, an SI): workstreams, rollout waves, milestones, critical path and governance.
- A recorded two-minute summary of the largest programme you have run.

**Mock interview:** a 30-minute "plan this deployment" scenario with a peer playing the hiring manager.

**End of week check:** you can explain the role in one sentence and plan a multi-team deployment out loud in 20 minutes.

### Week 2 — RAID, risk and dependency management

**Goals:** make risk and dependency management your strongest topic.

- [ ] Read "RAID, risk and dependency management" in [this file's model answers](./README.md#model-answers).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/README.md) sections A and B with [answers](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Read the ownership and prioritisation sections of [10 Behavioral answers](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md).

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A RAID log for your week 1 plan with at least ten specific entries (risks scored by likelihood and impact, each with owner, trigger, response and date).
- A dependency map showing which team needs what from whom, with the critical path marked.

**Mock interview:** a 30-minute scenario mock: "the customer's IT team slips a critical dependency by three weeks" and "how do you manage teams you do not control".

**End of week check:** you can explain avoid, reduce, transfer and accept with examples, and handle a slipped dependency with options rather than blame.

### Week 3 — Status reporting, governance and executive communication

**Goals:** handle the steering committee role-play and written exercises.

- [ ] Read "Status reporting and governance" and "Escalations and difficult situations" in [this file's model answers](./README.md#model-answers).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/README.md) section C with [answers](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Read the communication and conflict sections of [10 Behavioral answers](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md).

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A one-page weekly status report with RAG definitions, plus a five-slide steering committee pack for a red project with three options and a recommendation.
- An escalation email (under one page) following the issue, impact, tried, options, recommendation, decision-needed structure.

**Mock interview:** a 30-minute steering committee role-play where a peer plays an unhappy executive sponsor; record it.

**End of week check:** you can present a red project in under ten minutes, lead with the headline, and get a decision.

### Week 4 — Scope, change control, budgets and commercials

**Goals:** be confident on the commercial side of delivery.

- [ ] Read "Scope and change control" and "Budgets and commercials" in [this file's model answers](./README.md#model-answers).
- [ ] Read [09 Customer scenarios](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/README.md) section E (business acumen) with [answers](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Learn earned value (PV, EV, AC, CPI, SPI, EAC) from any project management reference and work through three numerical examples.

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A sample change request with impact assessment and options, and a short change control process description.
- A spreadsheet that tracks planned value, earned value and actual cost by week for a fictional project and calculates CPI, SPI and estimate at completion.

**Mock interview:** a 30-minute mock covering "the customer insists it was in scope" and "you will overrun by 20%".

**End of week check:** you can run a scope dispute calmly and explain your forecast with earned value numbers.

### Week 5 — Agile at scale, technical depth and go-live

**Goals:** cover the technical and methodology rounds.

- [ ] Read "Agile and scaled delivery", "Vendors, system integrators and partners" and "Go-live, handover and closure" in [this file's model answers](./README.md#model-answers).
- [ ] Read [08 Cloud, DevOps & integration](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/README.md) sections A, C and E with [answers](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Skim [03 System design answers](../../topics/03-system-design/ANSWERS.md) section A for how solutions are structured.
- [ ] Read [07 Data & SQL](../../topics/07-data-and-sql/README.md) section D (pipelines and data quality) for data migration risks.
- [ ] Read [12 Debugging](../../topics/12-debugging-troubleshooting/README.md) section B to understand incident triage.
- [ ] Read the official SAFe overview (Scaled Agile Framework website) and the Scrum Guide.

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A cutover runbook outline for a system you know: steps, owners, timings, go / no-go criteria, rollback plan and point of no return.
- A one-page hypercare and handover plan with exit criteria.

**Mock interview:** a 45-minute technical TPM-style mock: "draw the architecture of a deployment you ran and explain its delivery risks", followed by "explain SAFe and when it hurts".

**End of week check:** you can discuss integration and migration risk credibly with an engineer and plan a go-live weekend.

### Week 6 — Behavioural polish, company research and full mock loops

**Goals:** convert preparation into consistent performance.

- [ ] Re-read the "Behavioral" model answers in [this file](./README.md#model-answers) and [10 Behavioral answers](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md).
- [ ] Finalise six to eight stories in [the story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) with numbers.
- [ ] Research the target company's delivery model (professional services, partners, forward deployed teams) using [11 Company-specific](../../topics/11-company-specific/README.md).
- [ ] Prepare questions from [14 Questions to ask](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md).

**Hands-on deliverables:**
- A recorded 10-minute presentation of a past programme as a case study: context, plan, what went wrong, how you recovered, result, lessons.
- A one-page "first 30 days" plan for taking over an in-flight engagement at the target company.

**Mock interview:** two full mock loops (hiring manager, delivery scenario, steering committee role-play, behavioural) with different interviewers.

**End of week check:** you can do four rounds back to back with clear structure, and every story has numbers and a lesson.

## Daily routine

A realistic 90-minute day:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Warm-up: answer one scenario question out loud in two minutes (for example, "a vendor is late") |
| 30 min | Study the week's topic: model answers and shared sections; summarise in your own words |
| 30 min | Produce something: a RAID entry set, status report, change request, plan or runbook section |
| 15 min | Practise one scenario or story out loud with a timer and record it; review against the structure |
| 5 min | Update your story bank and plan tomorrow |

On weekends, use one longer block (two to three hours) for the week's hands-on deliverable and a mock.

After every mock, write down the two biggest gaps and fix them before the next one.

## Fast track for the repo owner's profile

- **Skim:** planning, status reporting and agile delivery. You already run sprint planning, release plans, monthly programme reports and onboarding plans and lead a squad of about four engineers, so focus on the customer-facing and governance framing rather than the basics.
- **Skim:** the technical round. As a principal engineer with about 15 years of experience and tech lead for several business-critical modules, you can explain architecture, integration and migration risk better than most delivery candidates; use this as a differentiator.
- **Biggest gap: external customer governance.** Most of your stakeholder work has been with internal teams (sales, finance, academic teams). Spend extra time in week 3 on steering committee role-plays with someone playing an external executive sponsor.
- **Biggest gap: commercials.** SOWs, change orders, fixed price versus time and materials, and earned value are probably new; do all of week 4 carefully.
- **Gap: scale and vendor coordination.** Practise answers about coordinating system integrators and several teams at once; connect them to your CRM/ERP integration work as your closest third-party experience, without overstating it.
- **Delivery scenario and cutover rounds:** use the bidirectional legacy MySQL and new Postgres bridge across six product lines (idempotency guards, ordering constraints, reconciliation) as your main programme story: phasing, dependencies, risk control and reconciliation map directly to cutover and data migration questions.
- **Legacy change and risk round:** the PHP 5.3 to 7.3 user migration is a good example of planning a change that must not break users.
- **Prioritisation and stakeholder round:** the order, subscription and commerce platform work (invoicing and payments tied to a cash-collection metric, a discount approval rules engine) gives stories about balancing sales, finance and engineering priorities.
- **Build-versus-buy or vendor evaluation questions:** use your contribution to the speech-to-text provider evaluation (buy-versus-build cost model, India DPDP Act constraints); say clearly that you contributed rather than led it.
- **Write all of these up** in [the story bank](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md) with your own confirmed numbers before mock interviews.

## Resources

- *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)*, Project Management Institute (risk, change control, earned value; basis of the PMP certification).
- *Managing Successful Projects with PRINCE2*, AXELOS (governance, stages and exception management).
- The Scrum Guide by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland (https://scrumguides.org).
- Scaled Agile Framework official site (https://scaledagileframework.com) for SAFe, ART and PI planning.
- *The Phoenix Project* by Gene Kim, Kevin Behr and George Spafford (flow, dependencies and IT delivery, in novel form).
- *Making Things Happen* by Scott Berkun (practical project and program management).
- *Accelerate* by Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble and Gene Kim (DORA delivery metrics).
- *Crucial Conversations* by Kerry Patterson and co-authors (difficult stakeholder conversations).
- Google's Site Reliability Engineering book, chapters on incident management and postmortems (https://sre.google/books).
- Certifications to consider as structured study: PMP, PRINCE2 Foundation, Certified ScrumMaster or Professional Scrum Master, SAFe certifications.
