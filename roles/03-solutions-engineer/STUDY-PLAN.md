# Study Plan — Solutions Engineer

For engineers moving into pre-sales (Solutions, Sales or Pre-sales Engineer); assumes about 10–12 hours a week for six weeks.

Read the role overview first: [README.md — The role](./README.md#the-role) and the [typical interview loop](./README.md#typical-interview-loop). The demo round usually decides the offer, and discovery and objection role-plays come close behind. Coding is light. So this plan builds one strong demo on the target product early and spends most of its time on talking to buyers, not on algorithms.

## Before you start

Spend 30 minutes rating yourself honestly. Skip or skim areas you rate 4 or 5.

| Skill area | Rate yourself 1–5 | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | | In a 30-minute call you uncover pain, business impact, current stack, decision process and a champion, and talk less than half the time. |
| Demo and storytelling | | You give a 20-minute demo built around the buyer's story, not a feature tour, and recover calmly when something breaks. |
| Product depth (target and competitors) | | You have used the target product's trial and the top two competitors, and can explain the core technical idea in two minutes. |
| Sales process (MEDDPICC or similar) | | You can fill in MEDDPICC for a hypothetical deal and say where the SE adds most. |
| Objection handling | | You answer price, build-vs-buy, incumbent, scale and security objections with a question first and honesty throughout. |
| POC scoping | | You write measurable success criteria and a mutual plan with an end date for a two- to four-week proof of concept. |
| APIs, auth and integration | | You can build a small integration with a REST API in an hour and explain OAuth, webhooks, SSO and SCIM. |
| Security, RFPs and compliance | | You can explain SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and data residency questions honestly and know when to bring in a specialist. |
| Behavioural / deal stories | | You have win, loss and "saying no" stories, framed around deals or internal adoption. |

## 6-week plan

Assumes ~10–12 hours a week. Pick the target product (or a product you know well) in Week 1; every later week builds on it.

### Week 1 — The role, the sales process and your target product

**Goals:** understand where the SE sits in a deal, explain why you want pre-sales, and start using the product.

- [ ] Read [Role and motivation — model answers](./README.md#role-and-motivation-1) and [Sales process and collaboration — model answers](./README.md#sales-process-and-collaboration-1).
- [ ] Learn MEDDPICC: [Explain MEDDPICC and where the SE contributes most](./README.md#explain-meddpicc-and-where-the-solutions-engineer-contributes-most).
- [ ] Adapt the motivation answers to pre-sales and variable compensation: [01 Recruiter and motivation — answers](../../topics/01-recruiter-and-motivation/ANSWERS.md#motivation--role-fit).
- [ ] Work Tier 1 of [COMMON-QUESTIONS.md](../COMMON-QUESTIONS.md#tier-1--asked-in-almost-every-loop).
- [ ] Sign up for the target product's free trial or developer account and complete its quick-start.

**Deliverables:**
1. A MEDDPICC sheet filled in for a realistic hypothetical deal with the target company.
2. A recorded 60-second "why solutions engineering, not software engineering or FDE?" answer.

**Mock:** a 30-minute hiring-manager screen with a friend: past projects, how you would work with an AE, why a quota-carrying team.

**End of week check:** you can describe the stages of an enterprise sales cycle and what the SE does at each, in two minutes.

### Week 2 — Discovery

**Goals:** ask better questions than the other candidates.

- [ ] Read [Discovery — model answers](./README.md#discovery-1), especially the [30-minute retailer discovery role-play](./README.md#discovery-role-play-a-vp-of-engineering-at-a-mid-size-retailer-has-agreed-to-a-30-minute-call-run-the-discovery).
- [ ] Read [how to technically qualify and when to walk away](./README.md#how-do-you-technically-qualify-an-opportunity-and-when-would-you-recommend-walking-away) and [finding a technical champion](./README.md#how-do-you-find-and-develop-a-technical-champion).
- [ ] Study [09 Customer scenarios — A. Scoping and requirements](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#a-scoping--requirements) and the [role-play tips](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#role-play-tips).

**Deliverables:**
1. A discovery question bank of 25–30 questions grouped by pain, impact, current state, decision process and timeline.
2. A one-page discovery call script for a buyer of the target product, with an opening, three "go deeper" follow-ups and a close.

**Mock:** a 30-minute discovery role-play with a friend playing a skeptical VP of Engineering; record it and count how often you talked for more than a minute.

**End of week check:** you can summarise the buyer's pain and business impact back to them in their words, and agree a next step.

### Week 3 — Build and rehearse the demo

**Goals:** a demo you can give in your sleep, with a backup.

- [ ] Read [Demo and presentation — model answers](./README.md#demo-and-presentation-1): [structuring a 20-minute demo](./README.md#demo-round-you-have-20-minutes-to-demo-our-product-to-a-technical-buyer-how-do-you-structure-it), [tailoring to discovery](./README.md#how-do-you-tailor-a-demo-to-what-you-learned-in-discovery), [when the demo fails](./README.md#your-demo-environment-fails-in-the-middle-of-a-live-demo-what-do-you-do) and [mixed audiences](./README.md#how-do-you-demo-to-a-mixed-audience-of-executives-and-engineers).
- [ ] Read [explaining the product to a non-technical buyer](./README.md#explain-our-products-core-technical-idea-to-a-non-technical-buyer-in-two-minutes).
- [ ] Study [09 — C. Communication](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#c-communication).

**Deliverables:**
1. A 15–20 minute demo of the target product built around one customer story from your Week 2 script: problem, "before", the product solving it, the result, the next step.
2. A screen recording of the full demo as a backup, and a two-minute non-technical explanation.

**Mock:** a 30-minute demo round with two people: one plays an executive who wants outcomes, the other an engineer who interrupts with "how does that work under the hood?".

**End of week check:** you have given the demo out loud at least three times and finish on time even with interruptions.

### Week 4 — Technical depth: APIs, integration, security and the hands-on exercise

**Goals:** pass the technical screen and the "build something in an hour" exercise.

- [ ] Integration fundamentals: [08 — A. APIs and integration](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md#a-apis--integration) and [B. Authentication, authorization and security](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md#b-authentication-authorization--security).
- [ ] Rehearse the [build-a-small-integration-in-an-hour exercise](./README.md#hands-on-exercise-build-a-small-integration-with-our-api-in-an-hour-and-walk-us-through-it).
- [ ] Read [RFPs, security and procurement — model answers](./README.md#rfps-security-and-procurement-1), especially the [SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR question](./README.md#the-prospect-asks-whether-you-are-soc-2-type-ii-hipaa-and-gdpr-compliant-and-where-their-data-will-be-stored-how-do-you-answer).
- [ ] Depending on the vendor: data platforms → [07 — A. SQL prompts](../../topics/07-data-and-sql/ANSWERS.md#a-sql-coding-prompts); AI vendors → [06 AI/LLM — C. RAG](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#c-rag-retrieval-augmented-generation), [E. Evaluation](../../topics/06-ai-llm/ANSWERS.md#e-evaluation-most-important) and the README's [AI vendor specifics](./README.md#ai-vendor-specifics-1).
- [ ] Two easy practical problems from [02 Coding — A](../../topics/02-coding/ANSWERS.md#a-reported-fde-coding-problems) in case of a light coding screen.

**Deliverables:**
1. A small sample app against the target product's API (or a public API like it), with auth, error handling and a webhook or pagination, plus a short README written for a prospect's developer.
2. A one-page security FAQ for the target company built from its public trust/security page.

**Mock:** a 45-minute technical screen with a peer engineer: product-domain questions, then 30 minutes extending your sample app live.

**End of week check:** you can explain OAuth, SSO and webhooks without notes and build a working API call from a blank file in under 15 minutes.

### Week 5 — POCs, objections and competitive positioning

**Goals:** turn interest into a technical win and keep your composure under pressure.

- [ ] Read [Proof of concept and technical win — model answers](./README.md#proof-of-concept-and-technical-win-1), including [writing POC success criteria](./README.md#write-success-criteria-for-a-proof-of-concept-of-an-observability-product) and [the open-ended 90-day pilot](./README.md#the-prospect-asks-for-a-free-open-ended-90-day-pilot-how-do-you-respond).
- [ ] Read [Objection handling — model answers](./README.md#objection-handling-1) and [Competitive positioning — model answers](./README.md#competitive-positioning-1).
- [ ] Study [09 — B. Difficult situations](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#b-difficult-situations) and [E. Business acumen](../../topics/09-customer-scenarios/ANSWERS.md#e-business-acumen).
- [ ] Use the top two competitors' trials or docs for an hour each.

**Deliverables:**
1. POC success criteria and a mutual action plan for one use case of the target product (dates, owners, exit criteria).
2. A one-page honest battlecard against the top competitor, plus written answers to the five objections: price, build vs buy, incumbent, scale, security.

**Mock:** a 30-minute objection panel: a friend plays the buyer, another plays your AE who tries to over-promise a roadmap item ([how to handle it](./README.md#the-account-executive-wants-to-promise-a-roadmap-feature-that-is-not-committed-what-do-you-do)).

**End of week check:** you answer each objection with a clarifying question first, and you never criticise a competitor by name.

### Week 6 — Deal stories, company research and full loop

**Goals:** polish behavioural answers into deal stories and rehearse the whole day.

- [ ] Read [Role-specific behavioral — model answers](./README.md#role-specific-behavioral-1).
- [ ] Behavioural: [10 — Customer focus](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#customer-focus), [Conflict and collaboration](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#conflict--collaboration), [Failure and learning](../../topics/10-behavioral/ANSWERS.md#failure--learning).
- [ ] Read [handing a closed deal to post-sales](./README.md#how-do-you-hand-over-a-closed-deal-to-post-sales) and [getting field feedback into product](./README.md#how-do-you-get-field-feedback-turned-into-product-changes).
- [ ] Company research: [11 — research checklist](../../topics/11-company-specific/ANSWERS.md#company-research-checklist-do-for-every-company).
- [ ] Prepare SE-specific questions: [14 — About the role](../../topics/14-questions-to-ask/ANSWERS.md#about-the-role) (add SE:AE ratio, how technical wins are tracked, team quota attainment).
- [ ] Tick off the role's [Prep checklist](./README.md#prep-checklist).

**Deliverable:** three deal-style STAR stories (a win, a loss, saying no) written into your story bank, each under two minutes.

**Mock:** a full mock loop: discovery (30), demo with Q&A (45), objection panel (30), behavioural with an "SE manager" (45).

**End of week check:** in the full loop your demo uses what you learned in the discovery round earlier that day.

## Daily routine

A realistic 90-minute weekday:

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–30 | Hands on the product: build one more piece of the demo or the sample app. |
| 30–55 | One role-play out loud (discovery opening, an objection, or a two-minute product explanation), recorded on your phone. |
| 55–75 | The week's reading: one README model answer, then answer a sibling question without notes. |
| 75–90 | Watch back the recording, write one improvement, and update your question bank or battlecard. |

Weekends: a full demo run and the week's mock.

## Mock interview schedule

| Week | Mock | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hiring-manager screen | A friend, ideally someone in sales or account management |
| 2 | 30-minute discovery role-play | A friend playing a skeptical buyer |
| 3 | Demo with interruptions | Two people (executive + engineer); record it |
| 4 | Technical screen + live extension | A peer engineer; interviewing.io or Pramp for the coding part if needed |
| 5 | Objection panel with an over-promising AE | Two friends |
| 6 | Full loop | Two or three partners; Exponent and similar platforms offer customer-facing mocks |

Give the demo out loud at least twice a week from Week 3 onwards, even when it is not the week's mock.

## Fast track for the repo owner's profile

- **Skip or skim:** the API, auth and integration study in Week 4 ([08 — A](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md#a-apis--integration), [B](../../topics/08-cloud-devops-integration/ANSWERS.md#b-authentication-authorization--security)); contract-first integrations and an API gateway with JWT auth and rate limiting already cover this. Spend the time on the hands-on exercise instead.
- **Skim:** SQL and data-platform questions, given deep MySQL and PostgreSQL experience.
- **Biggest gap 1 — demo and presentation practice with external customers.** This is the deciding round. Start the demo in Week 2, not Week 3, and rehearse it with strangers.
- **Biggest gap 2 — sales process and quota.** There is no evidence of quota-carrying or deal work. Learn MEDDPICC properly and prepare an honest "why a number attached to my work now" answer.
- **Biggest gap 3 — external buyer discovery.** Most stakeholder work has been internal (sales, finance, academic teams). Frame internal adoption as "selling" a solution, and say so plainly.
- **Discovery and POC rounds:** turning sales and finance requirements into the order/subscription platform (SKU catalogue, discount approval rules engine) is a good "uncovering real requirements" story.
- **Technical screen and integration exercise:** CRM/ERP integrations and a platform of about 13 services give credible depth on webhooks, auth and failure handling.
- **Objection "will it scale / is it secure?":** row-level access control and the large migration with idempotency and reconciliation give real examples to draw on.
- **Behavioural "saying no" and "deal you lost":** the speech-to-text provider evaluation (a contribution, not sole authorship), with a buy-vs-build cost model and data-protection constraints, maps well to a buy-vs-build objection from the buyer's side.
- Keep all stories in [../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md](../../my-prep/STORY-BANK.md).
