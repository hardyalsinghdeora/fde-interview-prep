# Technical leadership and behavioural

For principal and manager roles, the leadership rounds often decide the outcome: interviewers want evidence that you raise the output of a whole group, not just your own. Principal/Staff Engineer, Software Architect and Engineering Manager loops ask most, and Engineering Manager loops add questions about managing managers, hiring and performance. This file goes deeper on leadership; the general behavioural bank is in the topic file below.

## A. Fundamentals — must answer crisply
- [ ] How do you describe the difference between a senior engineer, a staff/principal engineer and an engineering manager?
- [ ] Tell me about a time you influenced a decision without having authority over the people involved.
- [ ] What does a good technical spec or design doc contain, and when do you skip writing one?
- [ ] How do you run a design review so it improves the design instead of becoming a debate?
- [ ] Tell me about someone you mentored. What changed for them, and how do you know?
- [ ] How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?
- [ ] Tell me about a decision you got wrong. What did you do when you realised?

## B. Working depth
- [ ] Two senior engineers strongly disagree on an architecture. How do you resolve it, and when do you make the call yourself?
- [ ] How do you decide between paying down tech debt and building features, and how do you sell that to product?
- [ ] How do you manage up when your manager or a stakeholder is pushing an unrealistic deadline?
- [ ] Tell me about delivering a project with heavy uncertainty: unclear requirements, new tech, shifting scope.
- [ ] How do you give hard feedback to a peer or a more senior person?
- [ ] How do you set technical direction across several teams that do not report to you?
- [ ] How do you run an interview loop, and what makes a good hiring bar for senior engineers?
- [ ] How do you onboard a new engineer so they ship something meaningful in the first two weeks?

## C. Senior / Principal depth (trade-offs, failure modes, scale, cost)
- [ ] Tell me about a time you said no to a senior leader or a big customer. What was the outcome?
- [ ] How do you know your technical strategy is working? What signals do you track?
- [ ] Tell me about a failing project you took over. How did you diagnose it and turn it round, or decide to stop it?
- [ ] How do you balance hands-on coding with leadership work, and what do you stop doing as scope grows?
- [ ] How do you build a culture of ownership and blameless learning after a major incident?
- [ ] How do you handle a strong individual contributor whose behaviour is hurting the team?
- [ ] (EM / manager-of-managers) How do you coach a first-time manager who is struggling?
- [ ] (EM / manager-of-managers) How do you run calibration and performance reviews across teams fairly?
- [ ] (EM / manager-of-managers) How do you know what is really happening in teams two levels below you?
- [ ] (EM) Tell me about managing someone out. How did you make it fair and humane?

## D. Scenario & design questions (whiteboard / take-home style)
- [ ] You join as principal engineer and find three teams building overlapping platforms. What do you do in your first 90 days?
- [ ] Write a one-page proposal to move from a monolith to services (or back). Who do you need to convince and in what order?
- [ ] Your team has missed three deadlines in a row. Diagnose the causes and present a recovery plan to leadership.
- [ ] A customer escalation needs a fix this week, but the right fix takes a month. How do you handle the customer, the team and the roadmap?
- [ ] Plan headcount and team structure for doubling an engineering org from 15 to 30 in a year.

## E. Hands-on checks (things to be able to DO)
- [ ] Write a two-page design doc for a real change with context, options, trade-offs, decision and rollout plan.
- [ ] Prepare 8-10 STAR stories covering influence, conflict, failure, mentoring, hiring, delivery and ambiguity, each with numbers.
- [ ] Write an architecture decision record for a past decision, including what you would change now.
- [ ] Draft a structured interview scorecard for a senior backend engineer role.

---

**Good answers show:**
- Specific stories with your own role, the stakes, the decision and measurable results, not "we did".
- Leverage: how you made other engineers or teams more effective, not just how you solved the problem yourself.
- Trade-offs stated openly, including what you gave up and why.
- Honest reflection on failures and what you changed in how you work as a result.
- Trust built across product, business and engineering, with disagreements handled directly and respectfully.
- For manager roles: clear thinking on people systems (hiring, feedback, performance, career growth), not only delivery.

**Prove it:** Pick a real decision from your recent work, or a well-scoped open-source proposal, and write it up as a design doc plus an ADR. Get two peers to review it as if it were a real design review, revise it, and use the review process itself as a story about influence and handling disagreement.
