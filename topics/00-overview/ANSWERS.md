# 00 — Overview: Model Answers

Questions: [README.md](README.md)

**How to use these answers:** These are reference guides, not scripts. For each story slot you get what makes a strong story, a STAR(+L) template with bracketed placeholders, and the questions it can answer. Fill the template with your own real experience, then practise saying each story out loud in 2–3 minutes.

**General rules for every story**
- One story should cover 2–4 checklist items; 8–10 well-rehearsed stories usually cover the whole list.
- Keep the split roughly: Situation/Task 20%, Action 60%, Result + Learning 20%.
- Say "I", not "we", when describing actions. Quantify results (time saved, revenue, latency, users, error rate).
- Have a 30-second version and a 3-minute version. Expect follow-ups: "What would you do differently?", "What did the other person think?", "How did you measure that?"

## Story bank checklist (prepare one story for each)

### Biggest technical achievement

**What makes it strong**
- Real technical depth you can defend for 10+ minutes of follow-up (architecture, trade-offs, why not the alternatives).
- Clear business or user impact, not just "it was hard".
- You owned a meaningful piece end to end. Bonus for FDE: it was built for or with real users.

**STAR template**
- **S:** At [company], [team/customer] had [problem] costing [time/money/risk].
- **T:** I was responsible for [designing/building X], with constraints [deadline, scale, legacy system, compliance].
- **A:** I [investigated/prototyped], chose [approach] over [alternative] because [trade-off]. The hardest part was [specific technical challenge], which I solved by [technique].
- **R:** [Metric before → after], adopted by [N users/teams], still running [duration].
- **L:** If I did it again I would [change], because [reason].

**Questions it answers:** "What's the most impactful thing you've built?", "Walk me through a project you're proud of", "Tell me about a complex system you designed", "Deep dive on a project from your resume", "What's your strongest technical skill?"

### Shipped something under extreme ambiguity

**What makes it strong**
- The goal, users, or requirements were genuinely unclear, not just "the spec had gaps".
- You show *how* you reduced ambiguity: talking to users, defining success yourself, shipping a v0 to learn, writing down assumptions.
- You shipped something real and iterated, rather than waiting for clarity.

**STAR template**
- **S:** [Stakeholder] asked for "[vague request]" with no spec, unclear owner, and [deadline].
- **T:** I needed to turn that into something shippable and useful.
- **A:** I [interviewed N users / shadowed the workflow], wrote a one-page doc defining [success metric + assumptions], got sign-off from [person], built a v0 in [time], and used feedback to [pivot/refine].
- **R:** Shipped in [time]; [adoption/impact metric]; the v0 revealed [insight] that changed the direction.
- **L:** I now [write assumptions down on day one / ship a thin slice earlier].

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a time you had to deal with ambiguity", "How do you start when requirements are unclear?", "Are you comfortable in a fast-paced, ambiguous environment?", "Tell me about a time you took initiative", "Describe a project where you defined the problem yourself."

### Worked directly with a customer/user to solve their problem

**What makes it strong**
- Direct contact with real users (external customer, internal ops team, clinicians, analysts), not filtered through a PM.
- You discovered the *real* need behind the stated request.
- You show empathy (understanding their workflow) plus engineering (you built the fix).

**STAR template**
- **S:** [Customer/user group] was struggling with [workflow pain], and had asked for [stated request].
- **T:** I was the engineer assigned to [solve/deliver] this.
- **A:** I [sat with them / ran calls], learned the actual problem was [root need], proposed [solution] with [trade-offs], built it in [time], and set up [feedback loop / training].
- **R:** [Time saved / errors reduced / renewal / NPS / expansion]. The user said [quote, if real].
- **L:** I learned to [watch the workflow before building / ask "what decision does this help you make?"].

**Questions it answers:** "Have you worked directly with customers?", "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer", "How do you gather requirements?", "Why FDE?", "Tell me about a time the customer asked for the wrong thing."

### Disagreed with a stakeholder/manager

**What makes it strong**
- A real, substantive disagreement (technical approach, priority, scope), handled respectfully.
- You used data or a quick experiment rather than opinion; you understood their constraints.
- The outcome is clear: either you persuaded them, or you disagreed and committed and explain why that was right.

**STAR template**
- **S:** [Manager/stakeholder] wanted [approach A]; I believed [approach B] was better because [risk/cost].
- **T:** We needed a decision before [deadline/milestone].
- **A:** I first asked about their reasons and learned [constraint]. I then [gathered data / built a quick prototype / wrote a comparison] showing [evidence], and proposed [compromise or option].
- **R:** We went with [decision]; outcome was [metric]. Relationship stayed [strong — e.g. they later asked me to review X].
- **L:** I learned to [raise concerns earlier / frame things in their goals].

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a conflict at work", "Tell me about a time you pushed back on your manager", "Disagree and commit example", "How do you handle a stakeholder who won't budge?", "Tell me about a time you were wrong in a disagreement" (if they were right).

### Failure / mistake and what you learned

**What makes it strong**
- A real failure with real consequences, owned fully (no blaming others, no fake "I work too hard").
- Fast recovery: what you did immediately to limit damage.
- A concrete, lasting change in behaviour or process (checklist, test, alert, habit) that you can show you applied later.

**STAR template**
- **S:** I [shipped/decided/missed] [X], which caused [impact: outage, wrong data, missed deadline].
- **T:** I needed to fix the immediate problem and stop it happening again.
- **A:** I [flagged it immediately to Y], [rolled back / fixed / communicated to customer], then ran a [blameless post-mortem] and found the root cause was [my assumption / missing test].
- **R:** Resolved in [time]; I added [guardrail], which has since [caught N issues].
- **L:** Now I always [specific habit], and I applied it on [later project].

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a time you failed", "Biggest mistake?", "Tell me about a time you received critical feedback", "What would your manager say you need to improve?", "Tell me about a project that didn't go as planned."

### Learned something new very fast

**What makes it strong**
- Short timeline (days, not months) and a meaningful result using the new thing.
- Shows your *learning method*: docs, reading source code, small experiments, asking experts, building a toy version first.
- Directly signals FDE "learning speed" — new customer domains and APIs every few weeks.

**STAR template**
- **S:** I had [N days] to deliver [X] using [unfamiliar language/framework/domain] I had never used.
- **T:** I needed to become productive enough to [ship / present / unblock team].
- **A:** I [skimmed docs for the core model], [built a throwaway prototype in hours], [read existing code / asked an expert targeted questions], and [kept a running notes doc].
- **R:** Delivered [X] in [time]; [impact]. Later became the go-to person for [topic].
- **L:** My repeatable approach is [build first, read deeply second / find the 20% that matters].

**Questions it answers:** "How do you learn new technologies?", "Tell me about a time you had to ramp up quickly", "What's something you taught yourself recently?", learning-round warm-ups, "Why do you think you'd do well with many customer domains?"

### Pushed back on a customer request / said no

**What makes it strong**
- You said no (or "not like that") for a good reason: scope, safety, security, cost, or because the request would not solve their real problem.
- You preserved the relationship by offering an alternative and explaining trade-offs in their terms.
- Shows judgment, which is a core FDE signal.

**STAR template**
- **S:** [Customer/stakeholder] asked for [feature/change] by [date].
- **T:** I believed it would [cause risk / not solve the problem / blow the timeline].
- **A:** I asked what outcome they needed, learned it was [real goal], explained [risk/cost] with [data], and proposed [alternative / phased plan]. I [confirmed in writing / escalated appropriately].
- **R:** They agreed to [alternative]; it delivered [outcome] and [avoided risk]. Relationship [stayed strong / they renewed].
- **L:** I learned that "no" lands better as "here's how we get you the outcome".

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a time you said no to a customer", "How do you handle scope creep?", "A customer demands a feature that's bad for them — what do you do?", "Tell me about a time you managed expectations", customer role-play rounds.

### Production incident you handled

**What makes it strong**
- Calm, structured response: detect, mitigate first, then find root cause.
- Clear communication to stakeholders/customers during the incident.
- Post-incident follow-through: post-mortem, fix, alerting, runbook.

**STAR template**
- **S:** At [time], [system] started [symptom], affecting [N users / customer / revenue].
- **T:** I was [on-call / the engineer closest to it] and needed to restore service.
- **A:** I [checked dashboards/logs], formed a hypothesis that [cause], mitigated by [rollback / feature flag / scaling], and posted updates every [N minutes] to [channel/customer]. After recovery I found the root cause [X] and [fixed it properly].
- **R:** Restored in [time to mitigate]; [impact limited to Y]. Post-mortem led to [alert/test/runbook] — no recurrence since.
- **L:** I learned to [mitigate before diagnosing / keep a timeline log during incidents].

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a time you debugged a hard production issue", "How do you work under pressure?", "Tell me about on-call experience", debugging-round warm-ups, "How do you communicate bad news to a customer?"

### Influenced without authority

**What makes it strong**
- You needed people you didn't manage (another team, a customer's IT, a senior engineer) to change something.
- You used understanding of their incentives, data, prototypes, or small wins — not escalation as the first move.
- Very relevant to FDE: you rarely control the customer's teams or the core product roadmap.

**STAR template**
- **S:** I needed [team/person] to [adopt X / prioritise Y], but they [had other priorities / were skeptical].
- **T:** Without authority over them, I had to get [outcome] by [date].
- **A:** I [learned their goals], [built a small prototype / gathered data from N customers], framed it as [benefit to them], [found an ally / champion], and [made it easy by doing the first part myself].
- **R:** They [adopted/prioritised] it; [impact metric].
- **L:** Influence comes from [making it easy to say yes / showing evidence].

**Questions it answers:** "Tell me about a time you persuaded someone", "How do you get product to build what your customer needs?", "Tell me about cross-team collaboration", "How did you drive adoption of a tool or practice?"

### Prioritized under competing deadlines

**What makes it strong**
- Two or more genuinely important demands colliding (two customers, a deadline plus an incident).
- An explicit prioritisation method (impact vs effort, risk, contractual commitments) and transparent communication about what would slip.
- Shows you renegotiated scope rather than silently working all night.

**STAR template**
- **S:** In [week], I had [deliverable A for customer X] and [deliverable B / incident] due at the same time.
- **T:** I couldn't do both fully on time.
- **A:** I listed everything, ranked by [impact / risk / commitments], told [stakeholders] early what I'd deliver and what would move, cut scope on [B] to [minimum version], and [delegated / asked for help] on [C].
- **R:** [A] shipped on time, [B] shipped [N days later / reduced] with stakeholder agreement; no surprises.
- **L:** I now [communicate trade-offs early / timebox].

**Questions it answers:** "How do you handle multiple customers at once?", "How do you feel about context switching?", "Tell me about a time you had too much on your plate", "How do you prioritise?", "Tell me about a missed deadline."

### Turned a one-off solution into something reusable

**What makes it strong**
- You noticed a repeated pattern (same integration, same script, same request across users).
- You generalised it at the right time — not too early — into a library, template, product feature, or internal tool.
- Measurable leverage: time saved per reuse, number of adopters. This is the core "FDE → product" signal.

**STAR template**
- **S:** I built [custom solution] for [customer/team A]. Then [B and C] asked for something similar.
- **T:** I saw we would keep rebuilding it unless it became reusable.
- **A:** I identified the common core vs the customer-specific parts, extracted [library/config-driven tool/feature], wrote [docs/tests], and worked with [core team/PM] to [productise / maintain] it.
- **R:** Next deployments took [N days instead of M]; used by [N teams/customers]; became [product feature].
- **L:** I learned the rule of [three: generalise on the third time] / to keep the config surface small.

**Questions it answers:** "How does FDE work feed back into the product?", "Tell me about a time you improved a process", "Tell me about something you built that others use", "How do you balance customisation vs scalability?", "Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?" (product thinking).

### Explained something technical to a non-technical audience

**What makes it strong**
- A real audience (executives, customer ops staff, sales, legal) who needed to make a decision.
- You adapted the message: analogy, visuals, focusing on outcomes/risk/cost instead of mechanism.
- Evidence it worked: they made a good decision, approved budget, or adopted the tool.

**STAR template**
- **S:** [Audience] needed to decide [X] but the issue involved [technical topic, e.g. model accuracy trade-offs, data migration risk].
- **T:** I had to explain it clearly in [meeting/doc, time limit].
- **A:** I started with the decision and its business impact, used [analogy / diagram / demo with their own data], avoided jargon, and checked understanding by [asking them to restate / taking questions].
- **R:** They [chose option / approved / adopted]; [outcome]. They later [asked me back / reused the explanation].
- **L:** I now [lead with the "so what" / prepare one diagram].

**Questions it answers:** "How do you explain technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders?", "Explain [RAG / an API / a database index] to a 10-year-old or to an executive", "Tell me about a presentation you gave", "How do you communicate with customer executives?", learning-round "teach it back" moments.

## Coverage map (which story to use when)

| If asked about... | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Impact, depth, resume deep dive | Biggest technical achievement |
| Ambiguity, initiative, startup fit | Shipped under ambiguity |
| Customers, empathy, "why FDE" | Worked with a customer; Pushed back on a customer |
| Conflict, feedback | Disagreed with stakeholder; Failure |
| Speed, learning | Learned something fast |
| Pressure, ops, debugging | Production incident |
| Cross-team, product feedback | Influenced without authority; One-off to reusable |
| Juggling, context switching | Prioritized under competing deadlines |
| Communication | Explained to a non-technical audience |
